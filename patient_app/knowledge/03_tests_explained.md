title: Tests your doctor may suggest
source: Summary of general patient information on lung nodule evaluation (American Thoracic Society and Fleischner Society patient materials)

## Low-dose CT (LDCT)
A quick scan that uses much less radiation than a normal CT. It is used for screening and for checking whether a nodule changes over time. You lie on a table for a few seconds while holding your breath.

## Diagnostic CT
A more detailed CT, sometimes with a contrast injection, to look closely at a nodule and the area around it.

## PET-CT scan
A small amount of a sugar-like tracer is injected before the scan. Very active cells, such as cancer cells but also inflamed tissue, take up more tracer and light up. It helps doctors decide whether a larger nodule needs a biopsy. You usually need to fast for a few hours beforehand.

## Biopsy
A small piece of tissue is taken to look at under a microscope. This can be done with a thin needle through the chest wall guided by CT, with a thin tube passed through the mouth into the airways (bronchoscopy), or during surgery. A biopsy is the only way to be sure whether a nodule is cancer.
