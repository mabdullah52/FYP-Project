title: Lung nodules - the basics
source: Summary of general patient information on pulmonary nodules (Fleischner Society and ACR Lung-RADS patient materials)

## What is a lung nodule?
A lung nodule is a small round or oval spot in the lung that shows up on a CT scan. Nodules are very common: many adults who have a chest CT have at least one. Most nodules are smaller than 1 cm.

## What causes nodules?
Most nodules are harmless (benign). Common causes are old scars from past infections such as tuberculosis or pneumonia, small lymph nodes inside the lung, and inflammation. A small number of nodules are early lung cancer, which is why doctors keep an eye on them.

## Why does my doctor want another scan?
The best way to tell a harmless nodule from a dangerous one is to see whether it changes over time. A nodule that stays the same size for about two years (solid nodules) is almost always benign. A nodule that grows may need more tests. A follow-up CT is therefore a normal, careful step - it does not mean the doctor thinks it is cancer.

## What makes a nodule more worrying?
Doctors look at size (larger is more concerning), growth between scans, shape (smooth edges are reassuring, spiky edges are more concerning), how dense it is (solid, part-solid or ground-glass), and personal risk factors such as smoking history, age and family history of lung cancer.
