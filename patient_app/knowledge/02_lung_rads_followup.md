title: Lung-RADS categories and what usually happens next
source: Summary of ACR Lung-RADS v2022 (American College of Radiology) management recommendations for lung cancer screening CT

## Category 1 and 2 - negative or benign appearance
No nodules, or nodules that look harmless (for example solid nodules smaller than 6 mm at the first screening). The usual advice is to continue yearly screening with a low-dose CT in 12 months.

## Category 3 - probably benign
Findings that are very likely harmless but should be checked once, for example a solid nodule of 6 to less than 8 mm at the first screening. The usual advice is a repeat low-dose CT in 6 months.

## Category 4A - suspicious
For example a solid nodule of 8 to less than 15 mm. The usual advice is a repeat low-dose CT in 3 months. A PET-CT scan may be used when there is a solid part of 8 mm or more.

## Category 4B - very suspicious
For example a solid nodule of 15 mm or larger. The usual advice is further tests soon: a diagnostic chest CT, a PET-CT and/or taking a tissue sample (biopsy), depending on the doctor's judgement.

## Important
Lung-RADS is a guide for doctors. Your own doctor considers your whole situation and may recommend a different plan. Always follow your doctor's instructions.
